Aperture

A brain-computer interface you wear behind your ear. I co-founded it and lead the product. We train the neural networks that read its signal, and I design the device and its charging case.

Role
Co-founder and product lead; mechanical design, electronics integration, charging case
When
June 2026 to now
Tools
PyTorch, Fusion 360, KiCad
Site
wearaperture.com

What I did

  • Trained compact neural networks in PyTorch on 20 public EEG datasets (917 people, 5,232 hours) to learn what two behind-ear channels can predict.
  • Designed the learning architecture: a 14-channel EPOC+ headset teaches a smaller model that sees only the behind-ear sites, evaluated on people and days the model never saw.
  • Built Aperture Lab, a Mac app that records the behind-ear sites and the EPOC+ in sync, runs guided sessions with timed cues, and trains and scores models on the recordings.
  • Designed the wearable, a 4.45 mm sealed pod on a weekly skin frame, and its charging case in Fusion 360, with both boards laid out and routed in KiCad.
  • Wrote the R&D plan to design freeze in May 2027, the first study’s protocol and consent form, and a research paper on near-ear sensing.

The models

What we asked

Before our own device records anything, we used recordings other labs have published to learn which states two short channels behind one ear can predict, in people a network has never seen.

What came through

Sleep, drowsiness, eyes-closed rest and physical activity. A channel behind the ear scored sleep as well as a clinical one, and gating the electrodes by head movement cut false readings during exercise from 23–48% to 1–11%.

What didn’t

Mind wandering, mental effort and stress were at chance once elapsed time and session were controlled for, even with a 14-sensor headset. So we don’t build the product on them.

Teacher and student

For our own data, a 14-channel EPOC+ headset recorded at the same time teaches a smaller model that sees only the behind-ear sites. Aperture Lab, the Mac app I built for it, records both in sync and scores each model on people and days held out.

The wearable

A sealed pod on a weekly frame

The electronics sit in a sealed pod, a crescent about 46 by 19 mm, that snaps onto a thin skin frame behind the right ear. The pod is 4.45 mm thick and weighs 3.77 g, and it’s kept and reused. The frame, a skin adhesive with two magnets of its own, is changed every week.

What it senses

One brain channel from two dry electrodes, with a third as reference: gold studs moulded into the face plate, under conductive-silicone caps the wearer snaps on and replaces. Behind a glass window, an optical sensor reads heart rate and blood oxygen. An accelerometer, a magnetic sensor that knows whether the pod is on its frame or in its case, and Bluetooth LE share the same board.

One curved board, no screws

A four-layer flexible circuit, 0.20 mm thick and curved to the head, carries 77 parts, laid out and routed in KiCad with no design-rule errors. Nothing is screwed or taped: every joint is moulded, glued with a heat-cured adhesive, soldered or held by a spring, and every glue line is drawn in the CAD at its real thickness.

Held on by design

Two pairs of magnets hold it on the head with 3.3 to 7.8 N, worst case to best. Their size and the face layout came from a search scored by a model of that hold: the layout before it missed four of the model’s five marks, and this one passes all five. Scripts then build the pod in Fusion 360 from the layout the search picked.

The charging case

A pebble for the pod

54 by 31 by 13 mm and about 13 g, matte black outside and grey inside, with one seam and the hinge hidden at the back. The lid holds only two small catch magnets; everything else is in the base.

It docks the way it’s worn

Two magnet bosses in the pocket go into the cups in the pod’s face where the frame’s magnets sit, so the pod snaps into the case just as it snaps onto the frame. They pull it in with about 4.2 N, and it still holds with 3.4 N once the spring pins push back.

Charging

Two spring pins press on the pod’s gold charging studs. The case’s own 180 mAh cell recharges the pod about 1.6 times, about four more weeks of wear between plugs, and the case charges over USB-C.

Its own board

A four-layer board, laid out and routed in KiCad, with a charger, a small controller and a status light behind a light pipe. It carries the same magnetic sensor as the pod, so the pins go live only with the lid shut and the pod inside, and charging stops when the pod is full.
